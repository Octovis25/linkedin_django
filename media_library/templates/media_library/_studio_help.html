🎨 Image Studio – User Guide

Build images, GIFs and videos for your posts. On the far left a narrow rail switches between five modes — only the tools of the current mode are on screen. Right at the bottom of that rail, set apart, sits 📖 Help: it opens this guide. The canvas sits in the middle, with the animation bar underneath, and uploads, assets and your finished outputs are on the right.

In this guide: the layout and the five modes · step by step: build a post · checklist line by line (Insert) · cut out & retouch (Image) · effects, magnifier, question web and spotlight (Effects) · GIF or video · templates · saving and posts · troubleshooting.

⭐ Fill a cut-out (transparent) background

  1. Go to mode Build and there to the Canvas section, row Colour.
  2. Click Cream, White, or the small colour box for any custom colour.
  3. The whole canvas fills instantly; your elements stay on top.
  4. Back to see-through: click None.

If a template loaded a background image (e.g. a teal layout), a colour sits behind it and stays hidden – click Image → Remove in the same section first, then pick your colour.

The five modes (rail on the far left)

  • 🗂 Build – Template (thumbnails, ➕ New, 💾 Save, Manage) and Canvas (Size, the Colour row for the background fill, Image: From selection / Remove, 🧹 Clear canvas). Clicking a template thumbnail while elements are already on the canvas asks first: keep your elements and take the background only, or replace everything with the template. So you can cut an image out first and pick the template afterwards.
    Size → 📐 Change… offers 1080 × 1080 (square), 1080 × 1350 (LinkedIn portrait), 1024 × 1536 (portrait 2:3), 1200 × 628 (link) and 1920 × 1080 (video), or ✏️ Custom size… for any width and height.
  • ➕ Insert – Text (+ Text, alignment; the size, colour box and Normal / Bold next to + Text describe the text you are about to create – to change text that is already on the canvas, use the bar above it), Text block (+ Block for heading + body, ✓ Check + text, ✓✓✓ Checklist), Shapes (arrow, lines, circles, rectangles, hexagons) and Badges (① Circle, ⬢ Hexagon, ▭ Banner – each with a number or text).
  • ✂ Image – cut out and retouch the selected image, see Cut out & retouch below.
  • ✨ Effects – two sections. Effects: 15 small moving pictures, one per effect – a click lays that effect over your image in a frame of its own. Marks: the word field and the Size slider, then 📍 Marker, 🖈 Stamp, 🔍 Magnifier, ❓ Question web, 🔦 Spotlight and the three badges. See Effects, magnifier, marks below.
  • ☰ Layers – Elements: every element on the canvas as a row; a click on a row selects it, ⬆ / ⬇ in the row moves it one step. Order: ⬆ Forward / ⬇ Backward for the selection, 🔗 Group / ✂ Ungroup. What sits higher in the list paints over what sits lower – that also counts for effect frames.
  • 📖 Help – below the separator at the foot of the rail. Not a mode: it opens this guide and leaves your current panel exactly where it was.

Brand colours are not part of a mode: they sit pinned at the bottom of the panel and stay visible in all five. The palette colours the selected text or shape and every new shape you insert – and also a selected effect frame, marker, stamp, question web or spotlight. + at the end of the row adds a colour of your own to the palette.

The three bars around the canvas

  • Above the canvas: ↶ Undo and ↷ Redo sit at the far left and are always there. Next to them the name of the selected element. Everything to their right depends on what is selected: W / H and X / Y with the 🔗 aspect-ratio lock, duplicate, flip, forward/backward, the six alignment buttons, delete — with more than one element selected, same size, distribute and 🔗 Group; with a group selected, ✂ Ungroup. With a plain text selected a Normal / Bold field appears here as well; it shows the weight that text actually has. Text blocks and checklists do not get one: there the weight is part of how the element is built. To change a single line of a checklist, split it first with ↕ Split rows. On the far right the view controls: grid, zoom (also Ctrl + mouse wheel over the canvas), ⤢ back to normal size and ⛶ large canvas. The small handles ‹ and › at the edges fold the side panels away and back.
  • Keys: Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redo, Ctrl+D duplicate, Del delete, the arrow keys move the selection by 1 px (Shift + arrow: 10 px), Esc ends a tool or a pick.
  • Below the canvas: 🆕 New, the Title field, the save button, ▾ Format, 📥 Download and ← Exit. What the save button does is described under Saving below. Opened from a post, ← Exit asks where to: ← To post #…, 🆕 Empty canvas, or Stay here — and the empty canvas is also how you let go of the post, so the next save goes to the library instead of onto it.
  • Animation bar (bottom): two groups you can fold open and shut; the head of each group shows how many rows it holds. Effects comes first: one colour palette for the effects, the switch Show frames on the picture (on to begin with), then one row per effect frame, magnifier, question web and spotlight. Motion follows: one row per element with its Motion, Speed and Start (when it begins). In the header: Tempo, Video length and ▶ Preview. All rows sit one under the other so you can time them against each other. Click the header to fold the whole bar away while you are building a still image.
    Every speed slider runs the same way: left slow 🐢, right fast 🐇.

Media panel (right, three tabs)

  • Upload – 📤 Upload image or 📐 Import SVG. Tick insert as one object to keep an SVG whole; without it ribbons, shapes and icons become separate elements. If the canvas is not empty, Import SVG asks Clear the canvas first? Your earlier uploads stay below as a grid: click or drag one onto the canvas, ✕ removes it from the list.
  • Assets – tick one or more folders (or a parent) to show their images; search within the selection. Click a picture or drag it onto the canvas.
  • Outputs – your finished Images / GIFs / Videos. A video tile plays when you point at it. Click to reopen; ✕ deletes the output, and with it the file in Nextcloud.

🛠 Step by step: build a post

  1. Start from a template. Mode 🗂 Build: click a thumbnail under Template (or ➕ New for a blank one). The background and logo load onto the canvas. You don't have to start here — if something is already on the canvas, the Studio offers to take only the template's background and leave your work in place.
  2. Set the background. In Build → Canvas → Colour pick Cream or White – or any colour in the small colour box. It stays the same in a GIF or video.
  3. Add your image. Use Upload image on the right, or tick a folder under Assets and click a picture. Drag it into place; scale from a corner handle.
  4. Cut it out (optional). Select the image, mode ✂ Image → Automatic → ✂ Remove background. Touch up with Erase / Restore, or recolour an area with Recolour.
  5. Add text. Mode ➕ Insert: + Block for a heading + body, or ✓✓✓ Checklist. Double-click to edit – one line per check, Enter adds a line, Ctrl+Enter is done, so a list can have four or five checks just as well. Change the colour with the brand colours at the foot of the panel.
  6. Add shapes/badges (optional). From Shapes an arrow, from Badges a ① Circle or a ▭ Banner with text to highlight something.
  7. Animate (optional). For movement of an element: in the bar under the canvas, group Motion, choose a Motion and a Speed. For an effect over the picture: mode ✨ Effects, click a picture, drag its frame where you want it. Then ▶ Preview.
  8. Save. Type a Title, then 💾 Save as… and choose Image (PNG), GIF or Video. It attaches to the open post and also appears under Outputs.

Tip: keep it to one clear message – a heading, a supporting line, one image and (at most) one highlight.

Concrete example (like the screenshot):
  1. Open a template → the cream background + Octotrial logo load.
  2. Tick the Logo folder in Assets, click the octopus figure, place it bottom-right, scale from the corner.
  3. If it has a background: select it → ✂ Image → Remove background.
  4. + Block → heading “Trust series with responsibility”, add a short body line.
  5. ✓✓✓ Checklist → type your three lines.
  6. Select the text and click petrol in the brand colours.
  7. Title it, 💾 Save as… → Image (PNG). Done.

Let a checklist arrive line by line

A checklist is one element: one circle, one tick and one text per row, glued together. That is handy while writing – and it means the list can only ever fly in as a block, because one element carries one Motion and one Start.

  1. Write the text first. Double-click the list, one line per check, Ctrl+Enter. Add the fourth and fifth line now – see the next paragraph for why.
  2. Split it. Select the list; in the bar above the canvas a button ↕ Split rows appears. One click turns every line into an element of its own. Nothing moves: each row stays exactly where it sat.
  3. Give each row its own Start. In the animation bar every row now has its own slider. With three rows: 0, 600, 1200. With five: 0, 400, 800, 1200, 1600.
  4. Check the length. The last row needs its second, so the video has to run longer than its Start – or move Tempo to the left, which slows both together.

Why the text comes first. After splitting, every row is a checklist of its own with a single line. Double-click still edits it, but Enter then adds a check inside that row rather than a new line underneath. A sixth check is a new element from then on.

One motion per element. A row can fade in or pulse, not one after the other. An element on a loop (Pulse, Float…) is visible from the first frame and merely starts moving at its Start – it does not appear. For a checklist that ticks itself off, pick Fade in, Zoom in or In from left.

Cut out & retouch — mode Image

Select an image on the canvas, then switch to Image. You make two choices instead of hunting through a list of buttons: which tool, and how far it reaches.

  • Automatic – Remove background works in one click on a coloured background: it averages the four corners, then eats inwards from the edges. On a white or light background it deliberately does nothing — see the box below. Remove checkerboard takes out a baked-in checkerboard pattern and leaves white content standing.
  • Tool – Erase (make transparent), Recolour (replace colour) or Select (mark an area first).
  • Applies to – Click an area (the default: click a spot, that connected area goes), Brush (wipe over it by hand) or Whole colour (click a colour, every matching area in the image goes).
Click an area Brush Whole colour
Erase click one area away wipe it away by hand that colour goes everywhere
Recolour recolour one area paint over it recolour every matching area
  • Recolouring works in two clicks: first click picks the colour you want (e.g. your petrol background — it shows in the little swatch), then click into the image. Pick the background colour to make part of the image blend into the background. For a different colour, press the tool button again.
  • Select gives you Rectangle or Free marking, then Delete area or Recolour area.
  • Restore brushes the original back where you erased too much; Done ends the tool. Esc also ends it.
  • The brush size and the colour box only appear for the tools that use them. The brush slider is not linear: the lower half covers 1–31 px, so the fine sizes are easy to hit. Far left is 1 px, far right 120 px.
  • Zoom in for detail work: Ctrl + mouse wheel over the work surface, or the 🔍 buttons in the bar above the canvas (up to 24×). Zoom in first, then work with a 1–3 px brush. Without Ctrl the wheel scrolls the page as usual.
  • With no image on the canvas the whole panel is greyed out — there is nothing to work on. Insert an image from Media and the tools come alive.
  • These edits are kept on save – also inside templates.
Worked example: removing a small blemish inside a graphic

A pale speck inside the lens of a pair of glasses, a few pixels wide. The trick is to repair it with exactly the colour around it, so nothing shows.

  1. Click the image, go to Image, and zoom in with Ctrl + mouse wheel until the spot is large on screen.
  2. Recolour, leave Applies to on Click an area, and click the clean colour right next to the blemish. The status line under the tools now reads 🎨 Colour #3DC2C2 – now click the area(s) to recolour. Note that hex value down.
  3. Click the blemish. If it disappears cleanly, you are done.
  4. Soft edges often leave a halo behind. Then press Ctrl+Z, switch Applies to to Brush, set the slider to 1–3 px, type the noted hex into the Colour field, and paint over the spot by hand.
  5. Done, then Save as… — the repair lives in the pixels, not in a layer.

Why note the hex down: switching the scope resets the picked colour. The little swatch goes empty and the Brush would paint with whatever is in the Colour field instead.

Do not use Erase here. Erasing makes the spot transparent, and what shows through is the canvas background — usually a different colour than the graphic around the blemish. You trade a light speck for a dark hole.

If a graphic carries the same blemish every time you insert it, it is in the source file, not in your edit. Repair it once in the asset and upload that again, rather than retouching it in every post.

Why “Remove background” sometimes does nothing

It protects white on purpose. Pixels that are bright and colour-neutral (brightness 200 or more, barely any colour) are never treated as background, so white content — lab coats, white icons, light areas in an illustration — survives. The price: on a white or checkerboard background everything is protected and nothing is removed.

In that case the Studio says so and leaves the image untouched. Use Erase → Whole colour and click the background: that removes the clicked colour throughout the image, without the white protection.

Effects, magnifier, marks — mode ✨ Effects

An effect does not hang on an element. It lives in a frame of its own that you drag over the picture – as large or as small as you need, and as many frames as you like. The frame itself is invisible: it never shows in the picture you save, only the effect does.

  • Add one: click one of the 15 pictures in the Effects section. The frame starts over the whole image – drag it by its surface, size it at its corners.
  • Find it again: Show frames on the picture in the Effects group of the bar is on to begin with: every frame is outlined, dashed. Pointing at a row lights up its frame and names it; ⌖ Select puts the handles on it, ✕ Remove takes it away.
  • Per frame in the bar: Effect (change it any time), Speed and Start. The Colour row above the frames paints the selected frame; auto goes back to the colours it was drawn in.
  • Stacking counts: a frame paints where it sits among the layers. Put the headline above the frame (mode Layers) and the effect runs behind it – the text stays readable.
  • Effects move only in the ▶ Preview, the GIF and the video. On a still PNG they do not appear – except the magnifier, the question web's threads and texts, and a still spotlight.
  • Drawings made before effects moved into frames are converted when you open them: an effect that hung on an element becomes a frame exactly around that element.

Marks – the section below the effect pictures. Two settings come first and apply to what you add next:

  • Which word: type it into the word field first, then click Marker, Stamp or a badge. Leave the field empty and the Studio asks for the word right after. To change it later: double-click the marker or stamp.
  • Size sets the size of the selected marker, stamp or badge, and of the next one you add. It grows and shrinks around its middle, so it stays on the word it marks. With a question web selected it takes all its question marks along.
  • 📍 Marker – a blinking sign you place yourself, e.g. a ? on the spot that needs attention. It blinks from the first second (Motion Pulse).
  • 🖈 Stamp – your word, stamped on at a slant. It fades in and stays, so the text underneath remains readable.
  • Colour: the brand colours at the foot of the panel.

🔍 The magnifier: a window, a glass and a way

The magnifier enlarges whatever lies under it. Its frame is the window it travels in; the round glass has a size of its own. While you edit, the glass stands at its starting point and a dotted orange line shows the way it will take. It is part of the picture, so it also shows in a PNG.

  1. Mode ✨ Effects → 🔍 Magnifier. A wide window appears in the middle.
  2. Drag the window over the text that should be read, and size it at its corners.
  3. In the bar, row of the magnifier, choose the Path:
    • Line by line – reads along the text, row by row (the default)
    • Left → right, then again
    • Back and forth
    • Wander freely
    • Stay put – it does not move
  4. Lens sets the size of the glass. If the glass gets bigger than the window, the window grows with it. Zoom sets how much it enlarges (1.2× to 3×).
  5. Speed is the time for one line: far left 20 s, far right 2 s – the value shows next to it, e.g. 7 s/line. A taller window does not make it hurry; the round just takes longer. Pause lets it rest at the end of each line so the enlarged words can be read.
  6. Next to Start you see how long one full round takes. ▶ Preview plays one whole round, up to 15 seconds – a longer round is cut off in the preview and the GIF, so then move Speed to the right or make the window flatter.

A magnifier made before the window existed has no path and stays where it is. Pick a Path and it starts to travel.

  • ❓ Question web – question marks you place yourself, tied together by threads that small sparks run along, like a web between the gaps of a process. Click Question web: three question marks appear, each with a sample text. Drag each one to its gap. In the bar under the canvas you change the texts, add a question (+ Question) or remove one, and choose: Threads (Web – each to its two nearest, Chain – in list order, None), Blink (One after another or All together), Text (shown when it blinks, always, or off) and Sign (?, ! or numbers 1 2 3). Speed runs left slow, right fast. The colour comes from the one palette of the Effects group or the brand colours. Threads and texts are part of the picture – they also show in a PNG; the blinking and the sparks play in the preview, GIF and video.

🔦 Spotlight: one element forward, icon and text together

Brings one element of the picture forward as a card, while the rest steps back a little.

  1. Click 🔦 Spotlight, then click the icon or its text in the picture. The Studio finds the element and lays the frame round both (on a light, calm background; otherwise the frame sits where you clicked – drag its corners to fit). Elements selected beforehand get their frame at once. Esc cancels.
  2. In the bar under the canvas, Spots: Just one or One after another.

Just one: Motion Still (part of the picture, also in a PNG) or Moving (it comes forward in GIF and video).

One after another makes a little film:

  • + Spot adds the next element, ✚ All elements takes every one the Studio finds.
  • Order: As clicked, Around ↺ / Around ↻ (starts with the middle, then goes round the circle) or Top to bottom. ↑ ↓ in a row fine-tune it, ✕ removes that spot.
  • Each spot has its own seconds; Pause is the gap between two spots; At the end: Whole picture or Start again.
  • One round shows how long the whole film runs. GIF and video stop at 15 s.

For both: Style Pop out or Ring only; Frame with or without; Where it is or to the middle; The rest softer, darker, blurred or unchanged, and How much; Heading stays clear above the dashed line (Top band) or steps back too; Zoom. The frame colour comes from the one palette of the Effects group.

🎬 Make a GIF or video

  1. Build the post first (image + text), exactly as above.
  2. Add movement. In the bar under the canvas, group Motion: give an element a Motion (e.g. Fade in, In from bottom) and a Speed. Repeat for other elements.
  3. Add effects. Mode ✨ Effects: click a picture (e.g. Network, Confetti). It lands in a frame over the whole image – drag and size the frame to where the effect should be. Several frames on one picture are fine.
  4. Stagger the elements. Start says when that element begins, in milliseconds from the first frame. It applies to Motion and to effect frames alike, including the looping ones (Pulse, Float, Spin…): until its Start is reached the element simply rests. Give three similar elements 0, 600 and 1200 and they come in one after another instead of moving in lockstep.
  5. Too fast? Use the tempo. Tempo, top right in the same bar, changes every Speed and every Start at once – left slow 🐢, right fast 🐇. 1× is as set, 0.5× half as fast, 2× twice as fast. Set the choreography with the relative spacing first, then slow the whole piece down without touching a single element. The automatic length grows with it. It is not saved with the drawing: reopening starts at 1× again.
  6. Preview. Click ▶ Preview to play it on the canvas. The preview shows the picture as it will be posted: no frame lines, no handles.
  7. Length. Set Video length (seconds) in the bar; empty = automatic. Looping effects run a few seconds. GIF and video stop at 15 seconds at most.
  8. Colour. A video keeps the colours you see here – the brand teal stays the brand teal on LinkedIn. No lighter shade is needed.
  9. Save. 💾 Save as… → GIF (smaller, autoplays in the feed) or Video (WebM, full size, better for longer clips). A GIF is scaled to 800 px on its longest side at 12 frames per second, to stay small for LinkedIn. It attaches to the post and appears under Outputs → GIFs / Videos.

Editing an existing template

  1. Mode 🗂 Build → Template → Manage, then Open in editor.
  2. Make your changes.
  3. Click 💾 Save under Template → choose Update this template. That overwrites the template, even if you changed its name.
  4. Want a copy instead? Choose ➕ Save as new template.
  5. If you only took a template's background, the Studio does not tie the canvas to that template. Saving then offers to create a new template instead of overwriting the original.

Saving and downloading

  • A title is required. Without one the field turns red and nothing is saved. The first save of a new drawing also asks for a name.
  • 💾 Save as… asks the first time: 🖼 Image (PNG), 🎞 GIF or 🎬 Video. From then on the button says what it will write – 💾 Save image, 💾 Save GIF or 💾 Save video – and saves without asking. For another format: ▾ Format or Shift + click on the button.
  • Opened from a post that already carries a picture, and animations were added since? Then the Studio asks for the format once more, so a quiet PNG does not drop them.
  • An output reopened from Outputs shows 💾 Save: it overwrites that output in the same format. 🆕 New cuts that tie.
  • 📥 Download saves a copy on your PC and changes nothing in the library: in the format chosen for saving, otherwise a GIF when something moves and a PNG when not. A video downloads as .webm.
  • Opened from a post, every save attaches to that post and appears under Outputs as well. Each post holds one medium: a new one replaces the old, and the old one goes back to Outputs – see the pictures further down.

The post banner

Opened from a post, a banner above the Studio names the post (📌 Post #…) and offers:

  • Files on the post: 📷 Image and 🎞 GIF load that file onto the canvas, 🎬 Video opens the video in a new tab. Greyed out means the post has none.
  • ✏️ Continue editing – loads the saved layout of the post again, with every element separate and editable.
  • 🖼 Background puts the post's picture in as the canvas background, 📐 Overlay lays it over the canvas as an element.
  • A post without a picture offers 📥 Image from another post (taken over as a copy) and ← To overview & status – set the post's status there, after saving.

Connect a Studio image with a post’s text

You write the text in the post, and link the image in one of two ways:

  • Image already made in the Studio: in Create Posts → Planner, open/edit the post, open the 📷 from Studio… dropdown at the image field and pick your finished image (from Outputs). It attaches to that post; text and image now belong together and go out together. (Videos: the 🎨 Studio outputs group in the video dropdown.)
  • Start from the post: click Open Studio on the post → the Studio opens linked to it. When you 💾 Save as…, the image attaches to that post automatically – no picking needed.

One post, one medium — in pictures

There are two ways a picture or a video ends up on a post, and they do different things. The post editor picks something that is already finished. The Studio builds something new. Both end at the same post — but only one of them is the right way for a file you already have.

A finished video belongs on the post through the post editor — the Studio cannot pick one. If the file sits in Outputs, attaching it moves it into the Planner folder; it is no longer in its old place.

Replacing never destroys. Whatever comes off a post — because you replaced it, removed it, or deleted the whole post — travels back to Outputs and can be hung on the next post. This holds for a file you once uploaded from your PC too. The one exception: a post whose status is Posted is left untouched.

The Studio opens in a tab of its own, so “back” has two meanings. With a post attached, ← Exit asks which one you mean. The empty canvas is also the way to let go of the post: from then on saving goes to your outputs instead of onto it. Without a post attached, Exit simply leaves and asks nothing.

What appears when you open the Studio from a post

In this order, and the first one that applies wins:

  1. The saved layout of that post — everything still separate and editable.
  2. No layout, but a picture on the post: the picture goes on the canvas.
  3. No layout, but a GIF: its first frame goes on the canvas, with a warning. Saving as an image replaces the GIF — the animation then goes back to Outputs.
  4. A video: nothing is put on the canvas. Flattening it and saving over it would cost the animation. Use the buttons in the post banner instead.

If a layout points at a picture that no longer exists, the status line says so — do not save over it, or you replace a file that could still be repaired. An empty canvas with nothing in the status line means there is genuinely nothing saved for that post.

Troubleshooting

  • Change not showing? Press Ctrl + Shift + R (reload without cache).
  • “No animations – nothing to export”: nothing moves yet. Give an element a Motion or add an effect frame, or save as an image.
  • GIF or video ends too early? Both stop at 15 seconds. Move Tempo or the Speeds to the right, or shorten the Starts.
  • Video colour darker on LinkedIn than here? That should no longer happen – note the post number and report it.
