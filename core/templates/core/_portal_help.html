📖 Portal – User Guide

This portal takes a LinkedIn post from the first idea to the published result: you write it under Create Posts, build its picture or video in the Image Studio, send it through Buffer, and see afterwards how it did under LinkedIn Statistics.

The tabs

  • Overview – the starting page. (short for now)
  • LinkedIn Statistics – how the published posts did. LinkedIn delivers its figures with a delay of about two days, so the last two days always look thin.
  • Web Analytics – visitors on the website, from Matomo. (short for now)
  • Create Posts – where the posts are written, planned and sent. Written out below.
  • Image Studio – builds the picture, GIF or video for a post. It has its own guide: open the Studio and click 📖 Help at the foot of the rail on the left.
  • Assets – logos, images and files to reuse. (short for now)
  • Data – looking things up: Posts Posted (what LinkedIn reports and what Buffer sent, side by side), Upload (the Excel import) and Collectives. (short for now)
  • DB Admin – the database itself. Administrators only.

Create Posts

The row below the tabs is the same set of posts, filtered: Planner is the planning view grouped by topic, and Draft, Review, Ready, Scheduled, Archive and All are lists by status.

The way a post goes

You move a post through the first four yourself, with the status field. From Scheduled on it is Buffer that decides, and the sync brings the answer back.

Writing a post

+ New post opens the form; the ✏ on a post opens the same one again.

  • Topic – which series it belongs to. It gives the post its colour in the planner.
  • Status – see above.
  • Date and time – when it should go out. The time is what Buffer is told.
  • Title – for you, to find it again. It is not part of the post.
  • Post text – what LinkedIn shows.
  • Image / video – see the next section.
  • Link – a URL. Careful: a link beats the media, see the rules below.
  • Internal comment – notes for yourselves, never sent anywhere.

Picture and video

Two ways, and they do different things:

  • Something finished – pick it in the post: 📷 from Studio… for a picture, and for a video the 🎨 Studio outputs or 🎬 Planner videos group, or a file from your PC.
  • Something new – open the Image Studio from the post, build it, and save. It attaches to that post by itself.

A post holds exactly one medium. A new one replaces what was there — and the old file is not deleted: it goes back to My outputs and can be used again. The Studio guide explains this with pictures.

Sending

The send dialog shows the text once more and asks for:

  • the channel – company page or personal account,
  • Post image too – appears when a picture hangs on the post,
  • 📅 Schedule on LinkedIn with date and time. Without the tick the post goes into Buffer's next free slot.

Post now hands it to Buffer. Copy text & open Buffer is the manual way out: it copies the text and opens Buffer, for when you want to do the rest by hand.

Three rules that decide what goes out

  • A link beats everything. If the post has a link, the link goes out and LinkedIn shows its preview — the picture or video stays behind. Leave the link empty if you want the medium to go out.
  • A video beats a picture. If both hang on the post, the video goes.
  • Videos go out as MP4. The Studio records in the browser, which produces WebM, and LinkedIn does not take WebM. The conversion happens by itself while sending, and the post is only handed over once the converted file really exists.

A post already in Buffer cannot be changed from here. Editing the text or the picture afterwards changes it in this portal only — Buffer still has the old version and will publish that. To really change it: withdraw the post (that removes it from Buffer too), change it, send it again.

After it has gone out

  • The Scheduled page asks Buffer once a day whether anything has gone out, and moves those posts on. 🔄 Check now asks straight away.
  • Published posts end up under Archive, marked Published. One that never went out and was put away by hand is marked Discarded.
  • The figures follow under LinkedIn Statistics — with LinkedIn's delay of about two days.

The other tabs are one line each for now. Say which one you need next and it gets written out.
